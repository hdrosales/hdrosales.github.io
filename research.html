Research

Collective phenomena in magnets

We use analytical and numerical tools to understand how frustration, topology and interactions shape the phases of magnetic materials, often in close collaboration with experimental groups.

Line 1

Magnetic skyrmions and topological phases

Skyrmions are particle-like, topologically protected spin textures. We study skyrmion crystals, fluids and glasses in ferro- and antiferromagnets, their stability under anisotropy and disorder, their interaction with itinerant electrons, and the topological Hall effects and magnon bands they produce.

Line 2

Frustrated magnetism and spin liquids

On kagome, honeycomb and triangular lattices, competing interactions prevent simple order. We classify classical spin liquids, study chiral spin liquids and order-by-disorder, and follow how these states turn into textured phases under a field.

Line 3

Strongly correlated, low-dimensional systems

Spin ladders and tubes, magnetoelastic chains and Hubbard models: magnetization plateaux, spin-Peierls-like phases and quantum phase transitions in one and quasi-one dimension.

Line 4

Classical & quantum magnetism, statistical physics

Collective phenomena in quantum and statistical physics, from Kawasaki dynamics and phase ordering to thermal Hall responses and spin-phonon coupling in spin ice.

Methods

Numerical simulations

Monte Carlo and parallel tempering, simulated annealing, Landau–Lifshitz–Gilbert spin dynamics, exact diagonalization and machine learning techniques to map detailed phase diagrams.

Collaborations

Theory meets experiment

Joint work with neutron scattering groups at PSI (Switzerland) and ILL (France) on materials such as MnSc₂S₄, TmAgGe and the spin ices Dy₂Ti₂O₇ and Ho₂Ti₂O₇.
